About Me

During my junior year of high school, I conducted a health education outreach project through hosting webinars for the local community on CRISPR therapies for Sickle cell anemia. Consequently, through reading upon the disease, I came to understand how the homozygous presence of one particular mutation in the DNA resulted in the dysfunctional hemoglobin protein. Due to my fascination to learn more regarding DNA and its regulatory mechanisms, I took upper-level stem courses during high school including AP Biology. As a result, I realized how meticulous regulation of gene expression is essential for the proper functioning of organisms through reading textbooks. Consequently, I began positing on the implications of misregulation of certain genes and how this can result in the onset of disease. As such, through the summer college-level course Stem Cell and Regenerative Biology, I learned more in this area and took a particular interest in how accumulation of mutations within a cell can result in the onset of cancer. Currently, I am a student at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill majoring in Biology. Through this coursework, I plan to explore more within the area of genetic regulation to deepen my understanding.
